# Cybersecurity and Digital Forensics: Why I Am Into It

## My interest in cyber

Cybersecurity is one of the areas I am most interested in. Along with web development, the cybersecurity courses were the ones I enjoyed most at university. I like the idea that every system has weaknesses, and that understanding how things can be attacked is the first step to protecting them.

## Digital forensics and the Autopsy lab

Inside cybersecurity, the part I find most exciting is digital forensics. In our Digital Forensics lab we worked with **Autopsy**, which is an open-source digital forensics platform used to examine disk images and recovered devices.

What I find interesting is the **entire process**, not just one step. It starts from collecting evidence at a crime scene, carries on through preserving that evidence properly, and goes all the way to analysing the digital devices that have been recovered. Every stage matters. If the evidence is not collected and handled carefully at the start, the analysis at the end cannot be trusted.

For me, forensics feels like solving a puzzle where the clues are hidden inside files, folders and data. You have to be patient and systematic, and you have to follow the evidence instead of jumping to conclusions.

## Why this fits my personality

My interest in forensics is connected to things I enjoy outside of class. I love **crime psychology games**, and one of my favourite books is **The Silent Patient**, which is a psychological thriller. So I am drawn to the investigative side of things: figuring out what happened, why someone did something, and what the evidence really shows.

That combination is probably the most specific thing about me: a focus on digital evidence gathering and analysis on the technical side, plus a love for crime psychology games on the personal side. Both are about investigation and understanding human behaviour through clues.

## How it connects to the way I work

The same mindset helps me when I debug code. A bug is like a small investigation. I check the error logs (the evidence), research what the error means, form a hypothesis, test it, and go step by step until I find the cause. I do not just change random things and hope it works.

## Where I might take it

Web development is my strongest practical skill right now, but cybersecurity and forensics are a strong interest that I want to keep exploring. As a software engineer, understanding security also makes me write safer applications.
